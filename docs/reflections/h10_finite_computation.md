## 1. The Real-World Incident: A High-Stakes Claim

On October 6, 2026, OpenAI deposited a research release on GitHub (`openai/math`), comprising 722 manuscripts across 372 mathematical result families generated by an internal frontier reasoning engine. Among these was an extraordinary proposition: a claimed negative resolution of **Hilbert's Tenth Problem over the Rational Numbers ($$H10(\mathbb{Q})$$)**.

Hilbert's Tenth Problem originally asked whether an effective algorithm can decide if an arbitrary Diophantine polynomial with integer coefficients has integer solutions:

$$f(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n) = 0, \quad x_i \in \mathbb{Z}$$

In 1970, the DPRM theorem (Davis, Putnam, Robinson, Matiyasevich) proved that integer Diophantine solvability is undecidable—equivalent to the Turing halting problem. However, the rational analogue remained a major open question for over half a century: *What if the variables are rational numbers ($$x_i \in \mathbb{Q}$$)?*

The October 2026 manuscript claims a Turing reduction ($$H10(\mathbb{Z}) \le_{\mathrm{T}} H10(\mathbb{Q})$$) bypassing classical geometric obstructions (Mazur's conjecture) through an effective sequence of finite tests. Yet, within hours of release, researchers noted a critical vulnerability: the paper is **completely unformalized in Lean 4** (unlike 42% of the release) and relies on unverified companion claims within the same repository.

Whether this specific proof survives peer review or fails, the underlying mathematical architecture exposes a physical reality that reshapes our understanding of computation.

## 2. The Fallacy of the Endless Void

Why are Diophantine problems undecidable in classical mathematics?

But if no answer exists, the search runs forever into the dark.
