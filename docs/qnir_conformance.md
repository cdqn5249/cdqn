## 4. The Conformance Oracle (Repair Step 4)

This section discharges the fourth step of the repair sequence on the byte-unchanged freeze of §1, the committed semantics of §2, and the committed accounting of §3. The operative instruction is the Step-4 row of the sequence, preserved verbatim: action — "Build a conformance oracle"; acceptance condition — "Assembler, validator, interpreter, and independent second implementation. Re-encode all examples and add adversarial tests for loops, calls, collisions, unresolved statuses, and budget boundaries." The inheritance register that headed this step in the forward map is fixed input and is preserved here verbatim so that the map of §5 can retire the row without losing it: "The frozen core as the re-encoding target; the F7 fatal-terminal and extension-boundary vector list; the `QO-22` token-lifecycle test list." Each register item is consumed at a named subsection (§4.2, §4.4, §4.4), and none is re-adjudicated.

The purpose is the limitation this program exists to close. The v0.2 review inherited the recorded limitation that repository bytes, benchmarks, and validator behavior were not independently reproduced, and the promotion criterion is written so that it cannot be met by assertion: "independent differential execution" and "every normative example validates" are checkable claims, and the conformance oracle is the mechanism that makes them checkable against repository bytes. The oracle's deliverable divides into a specification share and an artifact share, and the division is declared here rather than left silent (the same shape distinction §3.1 recorded for the ledger traces). This section delivers the specification share — the differential protocol, the normative requirements the four artifacts must satisfy, the vector format, the adversarial vector list, and the acceptance condition. The artifact share — the assembler, the validator, the reference interpreter, the second implementation, and the committed suite — is delivered by follow-on repository increments, one file at a time, each citing §4 and carrying its own declared version bindings, so that every conformance claim the promotion criterion names will be citable to committed bytes on both sides: the specification and the artifact that implements it. The oracle is software verifying a specification; it is not an execution substrate, and the Q4 trigger of the portal's Standing Governance Queue is untouched by it — the standing-queue notes of §5 record this. Under the {% include term.html id="no-implicit-rule" text="No Implicit Rule" %} discipline, every requirement below is a declared property with a declared checking site, and no conformance notion is resolved by silence, convention, or implementation accident.

### 4.1 The Differential Protocol

The protocol is the promotion criterion's "independent differential execution" made operational. Two implementations of the transition relation of §2.3 — the **reference interpreter** and the **second implementation** — execute the same encoded Intent under the same declared version tuple `V` (§2.1), and their outputs are compared byte-for-byte. Independence is declared, not aspired to: both implementations are separate codebases built from the committed bytes of §1–§3 and the normative requirements of this section and from nothing else — no shared code, no shared derivation, and no common input beyond this file's committed text and the declared vector format of §4.4. Conformance is decided by byte-equality of outputs, never by code review: an implementation whose outputs agree byte-for-byte is conformant, and one whose outputs do not is not, regardless of how its code reads.

The agreement surface is exact, and every point on it is a committed artifact of §2 or §3: (1) the canonical value encodings of §2.6; (2) the complete ordered transition-record sequence, each record carrying the F5 field list under the domain-separated encoding of §2.6; (3) the pre/post state roots at every step, computed by the state-root function under the declared encoding version (§4.3); (4) the terminal {% include term.html id="receipt" text="receipt" %} with its token itemization under the total terminalization function of §2.5; and (5) the reconstructed ledger of §3.1. A disagreement at any point is a conformance failure — registered as such, with the vector id and the disagreeing point named — regardless of which implementation is at fault; the protocol adjudicates the disagreement, never the implementation. The ledger check is mechanical by construction: §3.1 declared the ledger a derived record, reconstructed from the transition records' charged and refunded fields plus the terminal receipt's itemization, so the check that a published ledger equals the reconstruction of its own records involves no independent arithmetic anywhere — equal executions have byte-equal ledgers, and the oracle verifies exactly that. Every agreement point is a named check producing a typed verdict in the sense of §2.5 — pre-declared, sited, drawn from the declared vocabulary — and the protocol introduces no new status semantics of its own.

### 4.2 The Normative Assembler and Validator (F7, Step-4 Share)

**The assembler.** The F7 row of §1.4 requires every example to be re-encoded "against the frozen core only, by a normative assembler and validator." The assembler's declared requirements: its input is an example's source form over the nine frozen families; its output is the canonical 64-bit word encoding of §2.6; and equal inputs produce byte-equal encodings — the assembler is deterministic under the same discipline as the encodings it produces. An example that cannot be expressed within the frozen families is not forced into them: it becomes an extension-boundary vector of §4.4 under the deferral register of §1.3, so that the boundary is exercised exactly where the example cannot cross it. The re-encoding obligation is total over the corpus the artifact wave declares — the "all examples" of the acceptance condition — and its scope is fixed at that wave, citable by repository bytes.

**The validator.** The validator enforces mechanically what §2.1–§2.3 declare statically and what §3 gates: the four declared Intent-rejection classes of §2.1 — a deferred-family instruction, an unknown flag combination, a dangling `EXT` side-table reference, a missing profile reference; the static operand bounds of §3.2 as dispatch-time checks; and the budget-admission rule of §3.4, `W_budget ≥ B(P)`, at the execution's first attempted transition. Every validator rejection is a typed status token in the sense of §2.5 — pre-dispatch, typed, sited — and the validator invents no token outside the declared vocabulary; the named wrong mappings of Obj 5 stay excluded in validation exactly as in execution.

**The `B(P)` surrogate.** §3.4 declared that the specification defines `B(P)` and no algorithm, and registered the computational surrogate to this step. The declared requirement is directional and exact: the validator's surrogate must never under-approximate the declared maximum — the safe error direction is false rejection, never false admission — and an under-approximating surrogate is caught by the fuel backstop at run time instead of producing a silent over-burn (§3.4). The surrogate's algorithm, its complexity bound, and its adversarial vectors — the "budget boundaries" of the map's Step-4 row, received into §4.4 — are artifact-share deliverables; the polynomial-time question `QO-17` places in its calibration chain remains open and conditional, and the chain's own ordering, "Correct F1–F4 before calibration," is satisfied as of §3.6, F1–F4 being discharged there. The charge coefficients, the gateway charge, the base quantity, and the budget ride the validator as declared profile parameters under the profile version of `V` (§3.2, §3.6), never as built-in constants: no capacity constant is a validity limit of any rule (§3.5), and `QO-23`'s governance clause over coefficient ranges lands with the profile declarations that carry them, not in this file.

### 4.3 The Reference Interpreter and the Second Implementation

The reference interpreter implements the transition relation of §2.3 exactly as committed: one instruction per step; the partial-function discipline, at most one successor per configuration; the declared advance rule of the clock (§2.2, one tick per transition); the lifecycle verbs and the total terminalization function of §2.5; the F5 field list committed at every transition under the encoding of §2.6; and the admission refusal and fuel backstop of §3.4. It implements the accounting as §3 declares it — the ledger observes and gates the execution; it does not steer it (§3.3) — and no family's rule reads an accumulator in its implementation any more than in the relation.

The state-root function is the artifact share §2.6 named as the oracle's. The canonical configuration encoding is specified there — the value store in `(static value id, instance id)` order, the token pool in `(birth step index, type id)` order, the receipt queue in emission order, every component through the value encoding — and the concrete hash is an encoding-version parameter. The declared requirement is byte-equality under a declared version: both implementations bind the same declared encoding version, and their roots are byte-equal at every step of every vector; the hash's own algorithm and output width are declared with the encoding version at the artifact wave, which is all that F5's discharge needs and all this section requires. The second implementation satisfies the same requirements from the same committed text under the independence declaration of §4.1; nothing in this section requires or permits it to consult the reference interpreter's code, and its conformance is decided at the agreement surface of §4.1, nowhere else.

### 4.4 The Conformance Vector Suite (F7 / `QO-22`, Step-4 Share)

**The vector format.** A conformance vector is the declared tuple: the vector id; the encoded Intent under the canonical encoding of §2.6; the declared version tuple `V` — semantics, encoding, profile versions — with the profile's charge parameters; and the expected outcome — the complete ordered transition-record sequence, the terminal receipt with its token itemization, and the reconstructed ledger, each canonically encoded, together with the expected terminal outcome under the total terminalization function of §2.5. Equal vectors are byte-equal vectors; every vector is committed data, citable by repository bytes; and the suite is version-bound exactly as the artifacts are.

**The adversarial vector families.** The map's Step-4 row takes its scope in its own order, and each family is a declared obligation with a declared target. **Loops** — bounded structured control (family 5): back-edges at, under, and over their declared trip ceilings; nesting at and over declared depth ceilings; programs exactly admitted against `B(P)`, admitted with slack, and refused at admission, so that both the termination certification of §3.4 and the refusal path are exercised end to end. **Calls (extension-boundary)** — the deferral register of §1.3: a call construct yields the typed deferred-family rejection at Intent formation (§2.1, §2.3); the vectors test the boundary's closure, never extension behavior. **Collisions** — the collision surface of §2.6: vectors exercise the declared full-canonical-encoding equality and the typed handling of the surface; the collision bucket's resolution remains Step 5's (the F6 row of §1.4), so the vectors here fix the test obligation, not the bucket's answer. **Unresolved statuses** — the `QO-22` test list of §1.4, received unchanged from §2.5: success-after-recovery, unresolved debt at every exit, and multiple outstanding tokens; the fatal-terminal vectors of F7 and the family-9 row of §1.2 join here as first-class vectors — late sealing, fatal-before-halt ordering under the terminalization function, and the terminal receipt's itemization of unresolved debt at every exit. The Loop-3 repair (F12, v0.3.0-draft.5) closes the combinations: for every combination of {outstanding Tier S token class: `STATUS_NEG_EVEN_ROOT`, `EXPRESSION_TOO_COMPLEX`, `STATUS_DOMAIN_ERROR`} × {promoted, not promoted} × {fallback success, fallback repeated miss, fallback typed rejection} × {exit event: `HALT`, terminalizing birth, fuel backstop}, at least one vector exists; the promotion and no-promotion pair for `STATUS_NEG_EVEN_ROOT` is built on the declared initial status pool of §2.5 (the conformance birth path), and a success-after-recovery vector with an outstanding Tier O token fixes the declared residue rule — the outcome is unaffected and the token is itemized. **Budget boundaries** — the surrogate's adversarial vectors (§4.2): programs exactly admitted, programs refused at admission with the refusal execution's zero-posting ledger of §3.4, and vectors that exercise the fuel arithmetic and the backstop's declared role — an under-approximating surrogate is caught at run time, never a silent over-burn.

**The per-class ledger traces.** The F2 row of §1.4 requires per-class ledger traces to be published alongside the equation, and §3.1 and §3.6 registered the publication to this step: one ledger trace per rule class, published as conformance artifacts alongside the re-encoded examples, each mechanically checkable against the equation of §3.1 through the protocol of §4.1. The publication is this section's requirement; the equation it must satisfy is committed; and the distinction — the shape delivered at Step 3, the publication at Step 4 — is the one §3.6 recorded rather than left silent.

### 4.5 Step-4 Acceptance Condition and Non-Discharge Register

The acceptance condition closes on five deliverables, each with its discharge site declared: the differential protocol (§4.1); the normative assembler and validator requirements, including the error direction of the `B(P)` surrogate (§4.2); the reference interpreter and second implementation requirements, including the state-root artifact requirement of §2.6 (§4.3); the vector suite — format, the five adversarial families, and the per-class ledger traces (§4.4); and the re-encoding of all examples against the frozen core only (§4.2, §4.4). The specification share of each is discharged by this section. The artifact share is discharged when each named artifact exists as a repository increment that passes byte-level differential execution over the full suite and carries its declared version bindings — exactly then, and not before, the promotion criterion's "independent differential execution" and "every normative example validates" become checkable against repository bytes.

The registers are consumed, not amended: the F7 row of §1.4 — its re-encoding and vector obligations fixed as the requirements of §4.2 and §4.4, with publication at the artifact waves; the `QO-22` test list — fixed as the vectors of §4.4, received unchanged from §2.5; the family-9 fatal-terminal vector share — fixed as the first-class vectors of §4.4; the §2.6 state-root artifact share — fixed as the requirement of §4.3; the §3.1 per-class trace publication share — fixed as the requirement of §4.4; the §3.4 surrogate share — fixed as the requirement of §4.2 with the budget-boundary vectors of §4.4; and the forward map's Step-4 row — retired into this section's opening register, the map of §5 now listing only the steps still forward of the draft.

What this section deliberately does not deliver: **no artifact** — the assembler, the validator, the interpreter, the second implementation, and the committed suite are follow-on increments, each its own file, each citing §4, each budgeting its own companions under the same discipline; **no algorithm for `B(P)`** — the surrogate's algorithm and complexity bound remain artifact-share deliverables, and the poly-time question of `QO-17` stays open and conditional; **no collision bucket** (Step 5 — the F6 row; the vectors of §4.4 test the declared surface, not the bucket's resolution); **no full-trace roots, no artifact-lineage commitment, no verbalizer or extractor identity binding** (Step 5 — the F6 bucket, `QO-24` lineage, `QO-27`/`QO-28`, Obj 15); **no extension admitted or specified** (Step 6 — unchanged from §3.6); no numeric charge table and no profile values (unchanged from §3.6). The promotion criterion is untouched — "Candidate 1" is not styled a theorem, `R11'`, `R12'`, `R19`, and the `O(polylog)` statement remain conditional, the draft remains a Working Draft, and QnIR remains **[PENDING]** in the portal milestone. The standing-queue notes of §5 record what this increment does not fire: no queue trigger fires and no review loop opens by any increment of this draft, and the Steps 1–3 minimum that the Adversarial Review Program's gate names for submission candidacy remains met as of this version — a recorded state, not an opened loop. Under {% include term.html id="computational-consistency" text="computational consistency" %}, the validity claimed for this section is internal consistency within declared finite boundaries: the oracle specification is consistent with the frozen inventory of §1.2, the declared semantics of §2, and the committed accounting of §3, every declared choice cites its binding register row, and no canon file is contradicted or mutated.
