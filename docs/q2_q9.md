## 7. Algebraic Asymmetry: Positive Monoid vs. Negative Domain

While spatial reflection along axis $d_1$ is an algebraic involution ($\mathcal{I}_{d_1}^2 = \mathrm{id}$), **the behavior of Qn under multiplicative and dynamical operations is fundamentally asymmetric between the positive cone ($\mathcal{Q}^+$) and the negative cone ($\mathcal{Q}^-$)**.

```

 ┌──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┐

 │   Monoid generated by Primes.        │   ⊖ ⊗ ⊖ = ⊕ (Leaves negative domain) │
 │ Closed: Q+ × Q+ ⊆ Q+                 │ Open: Q- × Q- ⊆ Q+                   │

 │ Metric Work Footprint: +W            │ Metric Work Footprint: +W (Positive) │
 └──────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 7.1. Multiplicative Monoid Closure Asymmetry

The positive single-digit integers form the basis of a **free commutative monoid** under multiplication, generated uniquely by the indecomposable prime set $\mathcal{P} = \{ Q(2), Q(3), Q(5), Q(7) \}$:

$$\forall a, b \in \mathcal{Q}^+, \quad a \times b \in \mathcal{Q}^+$$

Conversely, the negative integers $\mathcal{Q}^-$ are **not closed under multiplication**:

$$\forall a, b \in \mathcal{Q}^-, \quad a \times b \in \mathcal{Q}^+ \quad (\text{Parity Rule: } \ominus \otimes \ominus = \oplus)$$

Multiplication within $\mathcal{Q}^-$ immediately exits the negative domain. Negative numbers do not form an independent multiplicative structure; they require the explicit inclusion of the reflection unit $\langle \ominus, 1 \rangle$.

### 7.2. Non-Linear Dynamical Asymmetry (The Collatz Phenomenon)

For any non-linear operator $\mathcal{F}$, spatial reflection does not generally commute with dynamical evolution:

$$\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{I}(X)) \neq \mathcal{I}(\mathcal{F}(X))$$

This is demonstrated constructively by the discrete Collatz mapping $T(n)$:

$$T(n) = \begin{cases} n / Q(2) & \text{if } n \in \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{even}} \\ (Q(3) \times n) + Q(1) & \text{if } n \in \mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{odd}} \end{cases}$$

- **On Positive Domain ($\mathcal{Q}^+$):** The $+Q(1)$ step acts as a damping perturbation against $Q(3) \times n$, driving all tested orbits into the unique global attractor cycle: $(Q(4) \to Q(2) \to Q(1))$.
- **On Negative Domain ($\mathcal{Q}^-$):** Substituting negative integers ($n = -m$ where $m \in \mathcal{Q}^+$) reverses the direction of the unit perturbation:
  $$Q(3)(-m) + Q(1) = -(Q(3)m - Q(1))$$
  The unit perturbation now acts anti-symmetrically, fracturing the dynamical trajectory into **at least three distinct, non-trivial limit cycles** (the 2-cycle, the 5-cycle, and the 18-cycle).

### 7.3. Thermodynamic Metric Asymmetry

In physical Layer 0 substrates, negative energy and negative storage do not exist:
- A negative artifact $\langle \ominus, k, z=0, r=Q(0), d_1 \rangle$ has an identical **positive physical footprint** as its positive reflection $\langle \oplus, k \rangle$.

  $$W(\ominus) \ge k_B T \ln 2 \gt 0$$
